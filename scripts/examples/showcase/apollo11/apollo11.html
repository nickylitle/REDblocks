Part 1 · Saturn V AS-506

The climb to orbit

Five F-1s, then five J-2s, then one. The engines come from equilibrium combustion chemistry; the masses, thrust levels and event times come from Marshall's flight evaluation report. One number was tuned: the pitch-over rate. Everything after that is a gravity turn and guidance, and the vehicle arrives in the same orbit as Apollo 11.

Part 2 · Lunar module Eagle

Powered descent to touchdown

The descent engine burns Aerozine 50 with nitrogen tetroxide. Apollo's quadratic guidance law, solved every 20 ms in the landing-site frame, steers it and throttles it: 10 % for 26 seconds, full throttle, then throttle recovery when the law asks for less than 57 %. Below 400 ft the sim flies the descent rates Aldrin called out, as Armstrong did. The dashed line is the automatic landing NASA planned had nobody taken over.

Under the hood

Engines from chemistry

Each engine is a REDblocks rocket_engine block: propellants burnt to chemical equilibrium in the chamber, then expanded through the nozzle with the chemistry shifting as it cools (the NASA CEA method). The block gives the ideal performance; one published rating sets how far below ideal the real engine sits. The descent engine's chamber temperature matches Cantera's to the hundredth of a kelvin.

REDblocks works out

  • Combustion temperature, exhaust speed, vacuum Isp and exit area of every engine (chemical equilibrium)
  • Thrust as the air thins, every 20 ms
  • The whole ascent: altitude, speed, flight path, range, dynamic pressure, Mach, acceleration, mass
  • When the S-IVB guidance shuts the engine down, and the orbit it leaves the vehicle in
  • The whole braking phase: steering and throttle from Apollo's guidance law, every 20 ms
  • When the throttle comes down, when high gate is reached, where and when Eagle touches down
  • Propellant burnt and left, delta-V, the mass on the Moon

Where it misses

  • Max dynamic pressure is 7 % low and 6 s early: standard atmosphere and a generic drag curve, not the day's weather or Saturn V wind-tunnel data.
  • At S-II/S-IVB separation the flight path angle is 0.06° against NASA's 0.61°.
  • The S-IVB cuts off 4.1 s late.
  • The braking phase dips early: 23,109 ft at 384 s against the plan's 24,639 ft. Apollo 11's own guidance targets were loaded into the computer before flight and are not in these sources, so the canvas aims at NASA's published high gate instead.
  • Throttle recovery comes 10 s late and the approach runs about 20 s ahead, so the sim's crew phase starts early and Eagle lands 28 s sooner than the real one.
  • Eagle had about 770 lb of propellant left; the sim has 1,352 lb. That gap is 3.4 % of the propellant burnt.

Left out on purpose: the translunar injection burn, the three-day coast and lunar orbit insertion. The page shows the two powered flights where the physics is hardest: climbing out of Earth's atmosphere, and landing on the Moon under guidance.

Run it yourself

Two files, one command each

pip install .                                              # from the scripts folder
simblocks run examples/showcase/apollo11/apollo11_1_ascent.yaml
simblocks run examples/showcase/apollo11/apollo11_2_landing.yaml
simblocks set examples/showcase/apollo11/apollo11_2_landing.yaml crew=0    # the automatic landing
python examples/showcase/apollo11/build_page.py            # rebuild this page
simblocks app                                              # open either canvas in the node editor

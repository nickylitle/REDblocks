Spinner vs spinner double hit

Does blade bending make a vertical spinner and a horizontal spinner hit twice in one engagement?
Double hit reproduced?
YES
fast double hits: H bending 19%, both bending 5%, rigid 1%

Findings

  • Second blade hit within 1.5 ms of the first: both blades bending 8/158 engagements (5.1%, 95% CI 2.6-9.7%), H blade bending and V rigid 30/159 (18.9%, CI 13.5-25.7%), V bending only 5.1%, elastic-only 0.6%, rigid 2/159 (1.3%). Same random engagements in every configuration.
  • Fast mechanism 1, H lag snap-back: 21 of the H-bending double hits come back after 0.10-0.17 ms (H arm in-plane half period 0.13 ms) with a second impulse of 4.3 N s median: the tooth is knocked back, springs forward and hits the same V tooth again.
  • Fast mechanism 2, flap and permanent bend: gaps of 0.6-1.4 ms when the V blade bends (H arm flap half period 0.63 ms; the V arm is too stiff to ring that slowly, so its part is the permanent bend that moves the tooth back into the path).
  • When the V blade also bends and yields, the H snap-back mostly lands inside the same contact: 8 fast doubles with both bending vs 30 with H bending alone.
  • Rigid blades: 2 fast double(s), all after a blade-on-chassis hit had slowed the V blade to 2390, 2904 rpm (gaps 0.98, 0.66 ms). No rigid engagement with both blades at speed double-hits inside 1.5 ms.
  • Context: in 9% of engagements a blade hits the other robot's chassis first (mostly the V blade on Flapjack's nose plate), slowing that blade. 7/8 of the both-bending fast doubles follow such a chassis hit; the H-bending-only ones mostly do not (13/30).
  • Nominal case (an H-blade snap-back double hit, V blade rigid; V at 12126 rpm, H at 12465 rpm when the blades meet): hit 1 8.2 N s, hit 2 6.83 N s after 0.15 ms; the H tooth is pushed back 15.1 mm in-plane (keeps 13.3 mm) and flaps 5.1 mm; 369 J into permanent bending.
  • Every run closes its energy ledger (worst 0.80%) and no contact point sinks past 36% of a half-thickness (no tunnelling through a blade).

Assumptions

  • H robot = Flapjack: 190 x 30 x 6.35 mm bar, 280 g, 600 J (11,360 rpm), blade mid-plane 15 mm off the floor, arm clamped at 12.5 mm radius. AR500 stands in for AR600/SR500 (not in the material catalog).
  • V robot = generic beetleweight bar vertical: S7 bar 120 x 25 x 12.7 mm, 12,000 rpm, axle 65 mm up, front of the blade moving up, arm clamped at 15 mm radius. All V numbers are placeholders.
  • Each arm bends in its first mode only: lumped tooth mass 33/140 of the arm mass on cantilever flap and lag springs with elastic-plastic hinges from the section's moment-curvature (beam_plastic_spring). Centrifugal stiffening of the flap (about 6%) and torsion are not modelled.
  • Contact: Hunt-Crossley with restitution 0.2-0.6, Coulomb friction 0.15-0.5, local stiffness 1e11 N/m per m of edge. Contact forces are not meaningful (no gouging or tooth plasticity); impulses and timing are.
  • Robots coast in at 2-4 m/s closing with drives and weapon motors off for the 40 ms engagement. No forks, wedgelets or armor geometry beyond simple chassis boxes.
  • Study data: the sampled inputs and ranges listed on the mechanism page, set in the job file. The nominal case is re-run and verified with the current sim (gap-driven big steps, early stop), which matched the fixed-step runs on 40 of 40 engagements.
  • Hits are blade-to-blade contact episodes; contact gaps under 0.1 ms are chatter within one hit. An engagement counts only if the sim runs 6 ms past its first hit.
